# Example: staffing and compliance-heavy decision

**Prompt:** Should our Cape Town retail store trade on Sundays through the Nov-Dec holiday season? We're currently closed Sundays.

**Placeholders supplied when asked:** current hours Mon-Sat 9am-6pm, average Saturday revenue ~R18,000, normal staff wage R55/hour, would need 3 staff for an 8-hour Sunday shift, no existing marketing material announcing Sunday hours.

---

**Active lenses:** the core four, plus HR (staffing/overtime is central) and Marketing (a new trading day only helps if customers know about it). Not CFO, this isn't a financing-scale decision.

**Quick answer:** Conditional yes, run a short trial before committing to the full season. Confidence 3/5.

## Evidence Snapshot

**From you:**
- Current hours: Mon-Sat 9am-6pm, closed Sundays
- Average Saturday revenue: R18,000
- Normal wage R55/hour, 3 staff needed for an 8-hour Sunday shift
- No existing signage or social posts announcing Sunday hours, no dedicated marketing budget

**From research:**
- Stats SA: November's share of combined Nov-Dec retail sales grew from 42.5% (2011) to 45.9% (2025), the Black Friday shift in spending is real and measurable
- Basic Conditions of Employment Act: staff who don't ordinarily work Sundays must be paid **double** their normal wage for Sunday work, not standard 1.5x overtime (Western Cape Government, Labour Guide South Africa). Since none of this store's staff currently work Sundays, this applies directly: 3 staff x 8 hours x R110/hour (double the R55 base) = R2,640 for the day, versus R1,320 for the same shift on a normal day.
- Gap: could not confirm Cape Town-specific municipal trading-hours restrictions, general SA retail hours appear to be set by individual businesses and lease agreements rather than a blanket law, worth confirming against the specific mall lease
- Gap: no store-specific data exists on whether an extra trading day grows total sales or just redistributes existing demand

## Panel Verdicts

**CEO (strategy):** Conditional. November is the genuine peak nationally, but the strategic case depends on whether extra hours capture net-new demand rather than reshuffling existing customers, that's untested.

**COO (execution):** Opposes as a full-season commitment, supports a trial. Sunday trading at double pay is a real, unavoidable cost under current staffing.

**PM (delivery):** Conditional, scoped narrow. Test 2-3 Sundays only, compare Sunday revenue against both a normal Saturday and the R2,640 cost, before deciding on the full stretch.

**Founder (speed):** Supports the trial. Cheapest test is 1-2 Sundays in the highest-traffic weeks.

**HR (people):** Conditional. Double pay is legally required here, not optional or negotiable at a lower rate unless the roster is permanently restructured to make Sunday a normal working day.

**Marketing (demand):** Conditional. There's currently no way for customers to know the store is open Sundays. A trial without signage or a couple of social posts first risks under-testing real demand.

## Cross-Examination

The COO's cost concern and the PM/Founder's trial-only approach resolve each other, a short trial caps the exposure to a known, bounded cost. The Marketing gap is a real blind spot the CEO and Founder's optimism assumed away: if nobody knows the store is open, a "failed" trial might just mean nobody heard, not that demand doesn't exist. Whether extra hours grow total sales or reshuffle existing demand remains the one thing nothing here resolves.

## Final Call

**Recommendation:** Conditional yes. Run a 2-3 Sunday trial in peak weeks, but only after putting up signage and a couple of social posts, otherwise the trial doesn't test what it's meant to test.

**Confidence:** 3/5, capped by no store-specific data on whether extra hours grow or just redistribute sales, and unconfirmed trading-hour restrictions.

**Why:** November is the genuine peak per Stats SA, but the BCEA makes Sunday materially more expensive than a naive estimate would assume, and an unadvertised trial can't be trusted either way.

**What would flip this:** If trial-Sunday revenue clears the R2,640 double-pay cost with a real margin, after the trial has actually been advertised, extend it. If it doesn't clear that bar even with signage up, don't extend past the trial.
